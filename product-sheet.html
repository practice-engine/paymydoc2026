Screen-by-screen

The complete patient walkthrough

Every screen in the production app, in the order a patient meets it — with what it does, why it exists, and the line a narrator would read over it.

1
Landing & Sign In

No password to forget

The front door is a single card. Patients enter the account number (MRN) printed on their statement plus the phone number on file — both must match the practice record before anything is revealed.

paymydoc.com
  • Two-factor identity match — a stolen statement alone is not enough to open an account.
  • Zero enrollment — no registration, username, or password is ever created.
  • Value stated up front — 0% plans, itemized detail, and card options are visible before sign-in.
  • Trust signals — HIPAA, 256-bit SSL, and PCI-DSS badges sit beside the form.
Why it matters: password resets are a major drop-off point in patient portals. Removing the account removes the reset.
Voiceover
"It starts with the bill already in your hand. Your account number, your phone number — that's the whole sign-in."
2
Multi-Factor Authentication

You choose where the code goes

Once the record matches, the app offers the contact methods already on file — text message or email — with every character masked except the last four digits and the domain shape.

paymydoc.com/verify
  • Masked destinations — (***) ***-4984 confirms identity without exposing it on a shoulder-surfed screen.
  • Patient-selected channel — no phone handy, or a shared landline? Send it to email instead.
  • Text message pre-selected — the fastest path stays one tap away.
Why it matters: MFA is what lets a no-password flow still clear the bar for protected health information.
Voiceover
"We'll confirm it's really you — by text or by email, whichever you have on hand."
3
Code Entry

Six digits, then you're in

A clean six-box entry field confirms the destination in plain text so the patient knows which device to check. A resend link handles the carrier delays that derail most one-time-code flows.

paymydoc.com/verify
  • Destination echoed back — "We sent a 6-digit code to (561) 247-4984."
  • Resend without restarting — the patient never loses their place in the flow.
  • Single-purpose screen — one field, one button, nothing competing for attention.
Voiceover
"Six digits. No app to download, no account to create."
4
Patient Dashboard

One number, three doors

The home screen answers the only question a patient arrives with — what do I owe? — then immediately offers every way to resolve it. The balance is the hero; the three payment paths sit beside it as equals.

paymydoc.com/home
  • Pay in full — the primary action, styled in solid green with the exact amount in the label.
  • Start a payment plan — always 0% interest, never buried behind a phone call.
  • Pay a partial amount — "pay what you can today" removes the all-or-nothing wall.
  • Active plan strip — any running plan and its balance surface right below.
  • Personalized greeting — time-aware, by first name, with the practice location in the header.
Why it matters: presenting partial payment and plans as peers of "pay in full" gives a hesitant patient something to say yes to.
Voiceover
"Here's your balance — and here are three ways to handle it. Pay it all, split it up, or pay what you can today."
5
Balance Detail

Every dollar, explained

The detail modal breaks the balance down by visit date and line item — procedure codes, product SKUs, and the full insurance math behind each one.

paymydoc.com/home
  • CPT and product codes — "10040 — Acne surgery" instead of an unexplained charge.
  • Full insurance waterfall — Billed, Patient Paid, Insurance Paid, and Contractual Adjustment on every line.
  • Patient Responsibility per line — the number that actually matters, called out in green.
  • Totals reconcile — line items sum to the dashboard figure exactly.
Call deflection: "what is this charge?" is a classic billing call. This modal answers it before the patient reaches for the phone.
Voiceover
"No mystery line items. Every visit, every code, and exactly what insurance did and didn't cover."
6
Pay in Full

Checkout without the friction

A two-panel checkout: the amount on the left, the card on the right. Saved cards are pre-selected, so a returning patient is one tap from done.

paymydoc.com/pay
  • Amount restated — no ambiguity about what is being charged.
  • Itemized breakdown still reachable — patients can verify without losing the checkout.
  • Saved card on file — brand, last four, and expiry, with Add New Card inline.
  • Amount in the button — "Pay $161.02" leaves nothing to interpretation.
Voiceover
"Pick your card. Confirm the amount. That's it."
7
Payment Plans

Affordability, set by the patient

Three preset terms — 3, 6, or 12 months — with the monthly figure calculated and displayed on each tile. Below them, a slider lets the patient name their own number and the app derives the schedule.

paymydoc.com/plans
  • Always 0% interest — stated on the hero, the tiles, and the slider.
  • Custom monthly amount — drag to any figure; the app shows the resulting term (e.g. $20/mo ≈ 19 payments).
  • Autopay disclosed before consent — the exact card and recurring schedule are spelled out in a confirmation band.
  • Card selected at setup — the plan and its funding source are locked in together.
Why it matters: a patient who sets their own monthly amount has committed to a number they already believe they can pay.
Voiceover
"Three months, twelve months — or slide to whatever fits your budget. Always zero interest."
8
Partial Payment

Pay what you can, today

A slider from a sensible floor up to the full balance. The remaining amount stays on the account with no penalty and no commitment — and the copy says so plainly.

paymydoc.com/partial
  • Live amount display — the figure updates as the slider moves.
  • Outstanding balance shown — the patient always sees what remains.
  • Explicit reassurance — remaining balance stays on the account; a plan can still be started later.
  • Same checkout panel — identical card UI as every other payment path.
Revenue impact: partial collection beats no collection. This screen gives a patient who can't pay in full a reason to stay.
Voiceover
"Can't cover it all today? Pay what you can. The rest just waits for you."
9
Confirmation

Proof, the moment it clears

A full-screen success state with the amount, a unique receipt number, the date, the card used, and both billing and provider addresses — everything a patient needs for an FSA or HSA claim.

paymydoc.com/confirmation
  • Traceable receipt ID — e.g. PMD-051126-091035, quotable to any billing rep.
  • Billed-to and provider blocks — the practice's name and address for reimbursement paperwork.
  • Unambiguous success — green check, large amount, no guessing whether it went through.
  • Download Receipt — one tap to the printable version.
Voiceover
"Done. Your receipt is ready before you've put your card away."
10
PDF Receipt

A document, not a screenshot

Download Receipt produces a print-ready one-page PDF carrying a PAID badge, the full transaction record, and support contact details.

Print → Save as PDF
  • PAID badge and official header — reads as a real financial document.
  • Posting expectation set — "allow one business day to appear on your provider statement" preempts a confused call.
  • Support details embedded — email, phone, and PCI-DSS notice in the footer.
  • Valid without signature — stated explicitly for employer and insurer submissions.
Voiceover
"Save it, print it, send it to your FSA. It's a real receipt."
11
Account Menu & Payment Methods

The wallet, under the patient's control

The avatar menu confirms who is signed in and exposes the full account surface. Payment Methods lists saved cards with one-tap removal and a standing promise: Pay My Doc never stores a full card number.

paymydoc.com/methods
  • Identity confirmed in the menu — name and account number, so nobody pays on the wrong record.
  • Manage Payment Methods — add or delete cards without contacting the office.
  • My Payment Plans — jump straight to any active plan.
  • Help & Assistance — billing support reachable from every screen.
  • Explicit Sign Out — important on the shared and family devices these bills get opened on.
Voiceover
"Your cards, your plans, your help — all one tap from anywhere in the app."
12
My Payment Plans

A plan you can watch finish

Active plans get a progress bar, a payment count, and the three numbers that matter: paid so far, remaining, and next charge date. The patient keeps every lever.

paymydoc.com/my-plans
  • Visual progress — "1 of 19 payments · 5% complete" makes a long plan feel finite.
  • Next charge date — no surprise debits.
  • Update payment method — a new card won't break the plan or trigger a failed charge.
  • Pay off and end early — settle the remainder in one tap, no penalty.
  • Payment history — a full audit trail of every charge.
Why it matters: self-service card updates head off the failed-payment cycle that quietly ends most medical payment plans.
Voiceover
"Track it, change the card, or clear the balance early. The plan bends to you."
At a glance

What the product does

Eight capabilities that define Pay My Doc — the short list for a slide, a sell sheet, or a training recap.

Passwordless Access

MRN plus phone, matched against the practice record and confirmed by a one-time code. Nothing to enroll in, nothing to reset.

Itemized Transparency

Charges broken out by visit, CPT code, and product, with the full insurance waterfall on every line.

Pay in Full

Saved-card checkout with the amount in the button. One tap for a returning patient.

0% Payment Plans

Preset 3, 6, and 12-month terms or a custom monthly amount, with autopay disclosed before consent.

Partial Payments

Pay any amount today. The balance stays on the account with no penalty and no lost options.

Instant Receipts

On-screen confirmation plus a print-ready PDF with a traceable receipt number for FSA and HSA claims.

Wallet Management

Add and remove cards, update the card funding an active plan, all without calling the office.

Plan Tracking

Progress bar, next charge date, full payment history, and a one-tap early payoff.

Built for protected health information

Security is not a footer badge here — it is visible on every screen a patient touches, because trust is what gets a medical bill paid online.

Two-Factor Identity

Account number and phone must both match the practice record before any balance is shown.

MFA on Every Login

A 6-digit one-time code to a masked phone or email the patient selects.

256-bit Encryption

Bank-level SSL in transit, stated in the header and reinforced at checkout.

PCI-DSS Compliant

Full card numbers are never stored. Saved cards show brand, last four, and expiry only.

HIPAA Aligned

Clinical detail is exposed only after the identity match and the one-time code both clear.

Masked by Default

Contact details are redacted on screen — enough to recognize, never enough to harvest.

For staff

Training notes & talking points

What front-desk and billing teams should know when a patient calls about the app.

"I don't have a login"

Correct — there isn't one. Direct the patient to the account number (MRN) on their statement plus the phone number on file. If either doesn't match the practice record, sign-in fails by design.

"I never got the code"

Have them tap Resend, or switch channels — the verify screen offers both text and email from the contact details on file. If neither arrives, the contact info in the practice record likely needs updating.

"What is this charge?"

Walk them to View Details on the dashboard. Every line shows the CPT or product code, what was billed, what insurance paid and adjusted, and the remaining patient responsibility.

"I can't afford this"

Two self-service answers: a 0% plan at 3, 6, 12 months or any custom monthly amount — or a partial payment today with the remainder left on the account, no penalty.

"My card expired"

Account menu → My Payment Plans → Update payment method. The plan continues uninterrupted; no need to cancel and rebuild it.

"I need a receipt for my FSA"

Download Receipt on the confirmation screen produces a one-page PDF with a receipt number, provider address, and a note that it is valid without a signature.

"Is my payment posted?"

The PDF states it: allow one business day for the payment to appear on the provider statement. The receipt is proof from the moment it's issued.

"Do you keep my card?"

Only brand, last four, and expiry. The platform never stores a full card number — the Payment Methods screen says so on the page.
